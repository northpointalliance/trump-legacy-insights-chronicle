Election Overturn Attempts & Voting Fights

From the 2020 post-election period through second-term passport and citizenship disputes, documented in courts, reports, and public statements.

What happened after the November 2020 election?

Donald Trump refused to concede, alleging widespread fraud without evidence upheld in court. His campaign and allies filed dozens of lawsuits; nearly all failed or were withdrawn. Public pressure targeted state officials (Georgia, Arizona, Michigan) and Vice President Mike Pence to reject or delay certification on January 6, 2021.

Documented steps included: the December 19, 2020 post calling for a January 6 protest ("Be there, will be wild!"); meetings with DOJ leadership to pursue fraud claims; the Eastman memo plan to have Pence block certification; and contact with state legislators about alternate slates of electors. The House January 6 Committee and DOJ special counsel investigations documented this multi-track effort to keep Trump in office despite losing the electoral vote 306–232.

What second-term fights involve passports, married women, and voting?

Passports & sex markers: On Jan. 20, 2025, Executive Order 14168 directed federal IDs, including passports, to reflect "biological sex at birth." Lower courts blocked enforcement for a time; on Nov. 6, 2025, the Supreme Court granted a stay allowing the State Department to enforce the policy while litigation continues (Trump v. Orr). Applicants who had used gender-identity markers or the "X" designation faced delays or reissued documents matching birth sex per State Department guidance.

Married women & name mismatches: Separate from the passport sex-marker case, proposed federal voting rules, including the SAVE America Act and Trump election executive orders requiring documentary proof of citizenship, would force millions of married women who changed surnames to produce extra name-change documentation to register or vote. PolitiFact and voting-rights groups documented the burden; the SAVE Act passed the House but stalled in the Senate as of early 2026.

Courts blocking voting EOs: U.S. District Judge Denise Casper permanently barred most of Trump's first election executive order, including proof-of-citizenship registration requirements, ruling it exceeded presidential authority under the separation of powers (PBS News, 2026). Other election-related orders faced injunctions in D.C. federal court.

TLDR: election and documentation disputes

The 2020–Jan. 2021 period combined litigation, public pressure, and Jan. 6 to challenge a certified loss. Second-term disputes extend to identity documents, voting access, and withholding federal grants from states that voted for Harris.
