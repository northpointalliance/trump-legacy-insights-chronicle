Federal Grant Cancellations in Democratic States

How the Trump administration admitted in court it terminated $7.6 billion in clean-energy grants based on 2024 electoral votes, not project performance.

Updated July 2026 · Thakur v. Trump and related litigation

What did the Trump administration admit in court about grant cancellations?

In a July 15, 2026 federal court filing, Department of Energy lawyer Jeff Novak conceded that 284 terminated clean-energy grants were included in an October 2025 cancellation batch “based solely on the political identity of the grant recipient’s state.” The admission came in Thakur v. Trump, a class-action suit by University of California researchers and other plaintiffs challenging the terminations.

Novak wrote that none of those October cancellations were driven by “programmatic, statutory, cost-reduction or performance-based” review. Democratic lawmakers called it corrupt abuse of executive power: federal aid withheld from states that did not vote for Donald Trump in 2024.

Sources: CNN, NBC News, The Guardian, Election Law Blog (July 2026 court filings)

How much money was canceled and when?

The October 2025 terminations covered roughly $7.6 billion in Biden-era clean-energy and research grants. The Energy Department had publicly announced 321 funding awards across 223 projects were cut, framing the move as stopping waste and projects that “did not adequately advance the nation’s energy needs.”

Court filings later contradicted that public rationale. OMB, led by Russell Vought, selected 284 grants from a larger DOE termination list. Vought posted on social media that “nearly $8 billion in Green New Scam funding” was being canceled to block “the Left’s climate agenda.”

TLDR: Public message: fiscal discipline. Court record: political geography.

Which states lost funding?

With one exception, every grant in the October 2025 termination tranche had a recipient location or place of performance in a state that awarded its 2024 electoral votes to Kamala Harris and has two senators caucusing with Democrats. Reporting identified 16 states where terminated projects were located.

Were Republican-leaning states treated differently?

Yes, according to the same July 2026 filing. DOE had recommended canceling more than 600 grants across both “blue” and “red” states after reviewing over 2,300 financial assistance awards. OMB terminated 284 grants in October 2025, almost all in Harris-won states with two Democratic senators.

The other 340 grants DOE had marked for possible termination were not canceled in October 2025 or afterward. Novak stated those remaining grants were tied to states that voted for Trump in 2024 or have at least one Republican-caucusing senator, even when DOE had previously recommended cutting them.

That asymmetry is central to the abuse-of-power framing: identical program category, opposite outcomes based on electoral map and Senate party control.

Why does this fit abuse-of-power tracking?

The Spending Clause and appropriations law require agencies to execute congressionally funded programs without using federal dollars to punish voters or states for electoral choices. When executives tie grant decisions to how a state voted, they convert appropriated funds into leverage over political opponents.

  • Parallels election-related retaliation and second-term funding fights
  • Overlaps policy shifts that benefit allies while harming opposition states’ economies and jobs
  • Litigation tracked on courts & delay pages as cases proceed

Sen. Patty Murray (D-WA, Appropriations) and Rep. Marcy Kaptur (D-OH, Energy appropriations) said the admission confirmed the administration “corruptly abused their power to kill good jobs and punish hardworking families because of their political views.”

What did officials say publicly before the court admission?

Energy Secretary Chris Wright and other officials described the October cuts as business decisions about viability and taxpayer value, not electoral payback. An earlier 2025 filing in related litigation acknowledged political affiliation could influence screening but did not state politics was the sole factor for the October tranche.

The July 2026 concession narrowed that ambiguity: for the 284 October terminations, politics alone determined inclusion. Reporting also noted DOE used keyword filters (diversity, gender, vaccine hesitancy, COVID-19) to flag projects for review, but the finalized October batch selection was electoral geography per Novak’s statement of facts.

Administration spokespeople have called media characterizations a misrepresentation; the quoted language above is from DOE’s own court filing.

TLDR: grants as electoral punishment

The Trump administration canceled $7.6 billion in clean-energy grants in 16 Democratic-leaning states, then admitted in federal court that the October 2025 cuts were based solely on whether those states voted for Kamala Harris and send two Democratic senators to Washington. Hundreds of similar grants in Republican-leaning states stayed funded despite DOE recommending termination. The case tests whether using appropriated dollars to reward allies and punish opposition states violates federal law.
