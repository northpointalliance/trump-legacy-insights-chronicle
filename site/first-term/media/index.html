Media, Fox News & Shifting Messages

How campaign rallies, cable news, and social posts shaped and documented first-term messaging from 2017–2021.

How did media coverage interact with the first term?

Donald Trump maintained a direct relationship with Fox News primetime hosts and used rallies as a primary messaging channel. Fact-checkers documented thousands of false or misleading claims during the term; positions on NATO, Syria, China tariffs, and COVID-19 response often shifted within weeks or months, with prior statements archived on video and social platforms.

After the 2020 election, posts alleging fraud escalated through November and December 2020 and were cited in the second impeachment inquiry following the Jan. 6, 2021 Capitol attack.

What does "changing what he says" look like in practice?

Examples widely cited in news archives include: praising then criticizing NATO allies; endorsing then condemning specific military withdrawals; claiming credit for stock records while blaming the Fed for corrections; and evolving COVID-19 statements from "under control" (Feb. 2020) to emergency declarations weeks later. Because many statements were on camera or Twitter/X, researchers could compare same-week contradictions, a pattern that continued beyond the first term.
