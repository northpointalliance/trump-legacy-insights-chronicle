Israel Policy, Donors & Media Framing

U.S.–Israel moves under Trump (2017–2021), the Adelson casino-and-media network, Netanyahu ties, and why many Israeli leaders and outlets called him "good for Israel."

Why did many Israelis say Trump was "good for Israel"?

Major policy shifts aligned with right-wing Israeli government priorities: moving the U.S. embassy to Jerusalem (May 2018), recognizing Israeli sovereignty over the Golan Heights (2019), exiting the Iran nuclear deal (2018), cutting aid to UNRWA, and brokering the Abraham Accords normalization agreements (2020). Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu praised Trump publicly and appeared at joint events; opposition figures still debated security and diplomatic trade-offs, but the governing coalition generally framed the relationship as historic.

That framing influenced domestic news coverage, policy wins dominated headlines in outlets sympathetic to Netanyahu, while critics focused on Palestinian status, annexation rhetoric, and long-term two-state prospects.

Who were the Adelsons and how did they connect Vegas, media, and Israel?

Sheldon Adelson (d. Jan. 2021) built Las Vegas Sands, casino resorts in Nevada and Macau, and became one of the largest Republican donors in U.S. history. He and Miriam Adelson funded conservative causes and candidates, including Donald Trump's 2016 and 2020 campaigns. Sheldon Adelson acquired Israel Hayom, a widely distributed free Israeli daily often seen as supportive of Netanyahu; Miriam Adelson became the paper's owner after his death and remained a major GOP and pro-Israel donor.

The Adelsons hosted Trump at Republican Jewish Coalition events, donated to inauguration festivities, and advocated for embassy relocation and hard-line Iran policy. Netanyahu stayed at Adelson properties during U.S. visits; the donor relationship was reported extensively in Haaretz, Reuters, and U.S. outlets as an example of aligned interests between a U.S. president, a major donor with Israeli media holdings, and an Israeli PM seeking U.S. backing.

How did Israeli news coverage differ from U.S. coverage?

Centrist and right-leaning Israeli outlets often led with security and diplomatic gains (embassy, Iran pressure, accords). U.S. outlets additionally emphasized domestic scandals, impeachment, and Palestinian reactions. Free-distribution papers such as Israel Hayom, under Adelson ownership during most of the term, were frequently cited by critics as amplifying pro-Netanyahu and pro-Trump frames; competitors like Haaretz and Times of Israel ran more skeptical analysis on annexation risks and democratic norms.
