DOJ, Ethics & Personal Attorneys

Justice Department leadership, personal lawyers in political scandals, and documented ethics conflicts, 2017–2021.

How was the Justice Department treated in the first term?

President Trump publicly criticized Attorney General Jeff Sessions for recusing from the Russia investigation; Sessions resigned under pressure in November 2018. Acting AG Matthew Whitaker and later William Barr led the department. The Mueller report (2019) documented contacts between the campaign and Russia and examined obstruction questions; Barr's summary framing was disputed by report authors.

Trump repeatedly called for investigations of political opponents and commented on ongoing cases, breaking norms that the White House not intervene in DOJ matters. The first impeachment (2019) centered on withholding aid to Ukraine while allies sought investigations into Joe Biden, involving personal lawyer Rudy Giuliani operating outside normal diplomatic channels.

What ethics and corruption issues were documented?

  • Emoluments lawsuits: plaintiffs argued payments to Trump properties from foreign governments violated the Constitution; courts dismissed some cases on standing; others mooted after he left office.
  • Impeachment (2019): House charged abuse of power and obstruction regarding Ukraine; Senate acquitted.
  • Impeachment (2021): House charged incitement of insurrection after Jan. 6; Senate acquitted after he left office.
  • Inspector General findings: various agencies documented misuse or pressure in specific programs; see GAO and IG reports catalog.
